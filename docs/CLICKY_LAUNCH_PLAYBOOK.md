# HII launch playbook: learn from Clicky without becoming Clicky

Status: founder-beta acquisition plan  
Audience: people who make and learn on a Mac  
Primary event: one person creates a useful HII workspace and completes one meaningful move inside it
Commercial event: a qualified person applies for a $500 founder activation

## What Clicky proves

Clicky does not launch by explaining an AI architecture. It launches one visible
behavior:

> Speak while looking at something on your Mac. Clicky sees the screen, guides
> you, or starts an agent.

Its public loop is:

```text
short native demo
→ instantly understood behavior
→ free Mac download
→ playful onboarding
→ visible user reactions
→ $20 everyday plan / $100 power-user plan
```

The important lessons for HII are:

1. Lead with a behavior someone can picture, not a category they must learn.
2. Show the product doing real work in the first few seconds.
3. Let people try something before explaining the full thesis.
4. Target by aspiration and behavior—making and learning—not only job title.
5. Make the founder and unfinished journey visible.
6. Publish many small demonstrations instead of one exhaustive product tour.
7. Keep the free entry obvious and put pricing after the product is understood.
8. Answer the frightening question—what can it see or change?—in plain language.

Clicky's public evidence also exposes cautions:

- Privacy is one of the first questions people ask about a screen-aware Mac app.
- Some users see many interchangeable AI companions and need a concrete reason
  to care.
- A second $20 AI subscription is a meaningful objection.
- Viral attention and early revenue do not prove durable retention.

## HII's target demographic

The initial segment is not every knowledge worker. It is:

> A Mac-based maker, learner, creator, designer, researcher, or independent
> operator who already uses AI, has real project material scattered across
> files and tools, and wants help finishing—not another blank chat.

They qualify for founder activation when:

- they can name one outcome they want within seven days;
- the correct result is worth at least $500;
- the work depends on information already on their Mac;
- they care which information the agent uses and what it changes;
- they will inspect the result and run the workflow again.

## HII's product sentence

> HII is a visual workspace for thinking, making, and working with AI.

Supporting sentence:

> Put notes, files, websites, images, models, chats, and tools in one place.
> Arrange the work like a visual canvas, ask HII to help, and keep the context,
> result, and proof connected.

The discovery wedge is still one real job. That is the first behavior to test,
not the permanent product category. Architecture, code, research, media, and
business are example workspaces inside HII—not separate product identities.

Do not lead with:

- control plane;
- artificial labor;
- AII;
- capability registry;
- MCP;
- knowledge graph;
- reusable skill promotion;
- credits or exchange.

Those are architecture or later benefits. They become meaningful after the
first successful job.

## The HII demonstration grammar

Every launch clip should tell one story:

```text
messy intention
→ visible source choice
→ one approved task
→ work in progress
→ finished artifact
→ open proof
```

The clip must answer:

1. What did the person want?
2. What information did HII use?
3. What did it make or change?
4. How can the viewer tell it is finished?

Use captions large enough to understand without sound. Show the artifact before
showing system internals. Keep infrastructure out unless it is the subject of
the clip.

## First five launch stories

### 1. From rough idea to portfolio artifact

Hook: `I gave HII a half-written idea and one folder.`  
Show: intention, approved folder, generated artifact, checks, receipt.  
Audience: designers, students, creative technologists.

### 2. Learn an unfamiliar project

Hook: `I opened a project I did not understand. HII showed me where to start.`  
Show: selected project, source map, plain-language explanation, next action.  
Audience: learners, career switchers, new contributors.

### 3. Improve one piece of real work

Hook: `I asked HII for the highest-value improvement I could finish today.`  
Show: current state, proposed improvement, approval, before/after, proof.  
Audience: indie founders, creators, operators.

### 4. Resume without reconstructing context

Hook: `A week later, the project still knew what happened.`  
Show: previous receipt, durable source links, next bounded task.  
Audience: people juggling several AI tools and projects.

### 5. The agent said it was done. HII checked.

Hook: `The answer looked finished. The receipt showed what actually passed.`  
Show: claimed result, verification, failed/passed check, corrected artifact.  
Audience: experienced agent users who already feel the trust problem.

## Fourteen-day founder launch cadence

### Days 1–2: front door and founder story

- Publish the one-sentence launch with a 30–45 second first-win clip.
- Link to `/learn`, not an unavailable installer.
- Post the founder thesis in plain language: HII exists to reduce the distance
  between an idea and a finished, inspectable artifact.

### Days 3–6: behavior demonstrations

- Publish one short demonstration per day from stories 1–4.
- Recut each demonstration vertically for Instagram/TikTok and horizontally for
  X, LinkedIn, YouTube, and the landing page.
- End every post with one question: `What is one thing on your Mac you want
  help finishing?`
- Reply to every substantive answer with a concrete proposed first task.

### Day 7: live build

- Run one real workflow live with explicit participant consent.
- Show the imperfect moments and the correction, not only the happy path.
- Invite five people to the guided beta.

### Days 8–12: proof and objections

- Publish one redacted receipt story.
- Publish a privacy explainer: what stays local, what can leave, and when the
  person approves.
- Publish a comparison framed around the job:
  `Chat gives an answer. HII keeps the sources, work, artifact, and proof
  together.`
- Do not attack Clicky, Codex, Claude, Notion, or Obsidian. HII can coordinate
  tools people already like.

### Days 13–14: conversion

- Share the first outside-user outcome with consent.
- Open five founder-activation slots.
- Use the offer:
  `Bring one valuable task. We will configure HII around it and stay until the
  first verified result works.`
- Do not introduce a subscription until repeated use identifies a responsibility
  people request and value.

## Channel roles

| Channel | Job |
|---|---|
| X | Fast product clips, founder learning, conversations with agent power users |
| Instagram / TikTok | Visual making and learning stories with minimal terminology |
| YouTube | Complete workflows, tutorials, and honest failure/recovery |
| Reddit | Technical explanations, privacy answers, useful build notes; never disguised promotion |
| LinkedIn | Independent-operator outcomes, workflow value, founder activations |
| Landing page | One sentence, real product, guided demo, beta/pilot conversion |

## Launch gates

Do not call HII publicly launched until:

- the canonical domain resolves;
- the web build is deployed and returns HTTP 200;
- `/`, `/learn`, `/activate?mock=1`, and `/pilot` work in a supported browser;
- a privacy page states the real transmission and storage behavior;
- the public CTA never offers an unsigned installer;
- the guided demo remains clear that no agent runs on the learner page;
- at least one real outside-user workflow has a consented, redacted proof story.

Do not charge for a downloadable Mac beta until Developer ID signing,
notarization, stapling, Gatekeeper, installation, and uninstall checks pass.

## Measures

Track the funnel:

```text
demo view
→ /learn visit
→ shaped first task
→ safe activation demo
→ beta request
→ guided activation
→ first verified receipt
→ second receipt within seven days
```

Primary measures:

- percentage of landing visitors who open `/learn`;
- percentage of learners who shape a task;
- number of beta requests containing a concrete task;
- time to first verified receipt;
- founder minutes per activation;
- second receipt within seven days;
- which promise caused the person to act: finishing, context, control, proof, or
  repeatability.

Follower count, impressions, and likes are distribution signals. They are not
product validation.

## Current external blockers

At the July 29, 2026 audit:

- `humaninformationinterface.com` did not resolve in DNS;
- the signed/notarized public Mac installer was still preparing;
- no current outside-user proof story was verified in this audit.

The truthful top-of-funnel action is therefore `/learn` plus beta recruitment.
The installer and paid activation should open only when their respective gates
are proven.
